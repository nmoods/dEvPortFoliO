# Black River Gorges National Park trails

## Overview
Black River Gorges National Park protects the largest area of native forest left in Mauritius, in the south-west of the island. It is home to rare endemic birds such as the echo parakeet, the pink pigeon and the Mauritius kestrel. Entry to the park and its trails is free.

## Entrances
There are two official entrances with visitor centres: Pétrin, on the plateau, and Rivière Noire, at the bottom of the gorges on the west side. Rangers there can advise on current trail conditions, and trail maps are available. Some sources say the park gates close at about 5 pm.

## Trails
The Macchabée forest loop starts from Pétrin and runs through some of the best native forest on the island. Its length varies by variant, with sources quoting anywhere from about 8 to 14 km. The Parakeet Trail is a much harder one-way descent of roughly 6 to 8 km through steep, technical terrain, taking most people three to four hours. Gentler options include short walks to viewpoints such as Alexandra Falls and the Black River Gorges viewpoint.

## Difficulty
Difficulty ranges from easy viewpoint strolls to hard, technical descents. Trails are not always well marked, so check the route and conditions at a visitor centre before you set off.

## When to go
May to November is the best season, with fewer muddy paths. The plateau is often misty and cooler than the coast, so bring a light rain jacket.

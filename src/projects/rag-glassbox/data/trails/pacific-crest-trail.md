# Pacific Crest Trail

## Overview
The Pacific Crest Trail, or PCT, runs about 4,265 km (2,650 miles) from the Mexican border near Campo, California, to the Canadian border in Manning Park, British Columbia. It crosses California, Oregon and Washington. Thru-hikers who walk it end to end in one season typically take four and a half to six months, but most visitors walk shorter sections.

## Route
The southern part crosses the desert of Southern California, where water sources can be 30 km apart. It then enters the High Sierra, passing through Sequoia, Kings Canyon and Yosemite and near Mount Whitney, before continuing north through volcanic country around Lassen. Oregon is the fastest state, with gentle forest walking past Crater Lake. Washington finishes with the steep, rugged North Cascades.

## When to go
Northbound thru-hikers usually start in April or early May, timing their arrival in the Sierra for after the worst of the snow melts in June. They aim to reach Canada in September, before early autumn storms in Washington. Section hikers can choose the best month for each region, from spring in the desert to late summer in the mountains.

## Where to stay
This is a camping trail: hikers carry a tent or shelter and sleep at established sites or durable spots along the route. Towns near the trail offer motels and hostels for rest days, and some communities have long traditions of hosting hikers.

## Difficulty
The PCT is very demanding because of its length rather than its technical terrain. Hikers face heat and long water carries in the desert, snow, high passes and river crossings in the Sierra, and fatigue over months. Navigation, food planning and gear choices matter as much as fitness.

## Practicalities
A free long-distance permit from the Pacific Crest Trail Association is needed for trips over 500 miles, with a limited number of daily start dates from the Mexican border. A separate permit is required to enter Canada on foot. Hikers resupply by walking or hitching into towns or by mailing boxes of food to post offices along the way. A bear canister is required in parts of the Sierra.

# Camino Francés

## Overview
The Camino Francés is the most travelled route of the Camino de Santiago, a pilgrimage network that ends at the cathedral of Santiago de Compostela in Galicia. It covers about 780 km from Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port in the French Basque Country and usually takes four to five weeks. Walkers come for religious, cultural and personal reasons, and the shared rhythm of the pilgrim community is part of the experience.

## Route
The first day is the hardest: a climb over the Pyrenees to Roncesvalles in Spain. The route then passes through Pamplona, the wine region of La Rioja and the cathedral city of Burgos. Between Burgos and León lies the Meseta, a high, flat plateau of wheat fields with long, shadeless stretches. After León and Astorga the path climbs to the Cruz de Ferro and later to O Cebreiro, the gateway to green, rainy Galicia, before the final days to Santiago.

## When to go
Spring and autumn are the most pleasant seasons. In July and August the Meseta can be extremely hot and the albergues are crowded. Winter is quiet but many accommodations close and the Pyrenean crossing can be closed by snow, with a lower road alternative via Valcarlos.

## Where to stay
Pilgrims stay in albergues, hostels reserved for walkers and cyclists, which are cheap and usually work on a first-come basis. Municipal and parish albergues sometimes ask only for a donation. Private albergues, pensions and hotels can be booked ahead. To use pilgrim albergues you need a credencial, the pilgrim passport.

## Difficulty
The terrain is easy: wide tracks, village streets and some road walking. The difficulty is cumulative, from walking 20 to 30 km every day for weeks. Blisters and tendon strain are the most common reasons people stop.

## Practicalities
The credencial is stamped at albergues, churches and cafés along the way. To receive the Compostela certificate in Santiago you must walk at least the last 100 km, which is why Sarria is a popular starting town, and collect at least two stamps per day on that final stretch. Yellow arrows and scallop shells mark the whole route.

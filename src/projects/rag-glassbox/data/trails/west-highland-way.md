# West Highland Way

## Overview
The West Highland Way runs about 154 km (96 miles) from Milngavie, on the edge of Glasgow, to Fort William in the Scottish Highlands. Opened in 1980, it was Scotland's first officially designated long-distance route. Most people complete it in seven or eight days, walking south to north so the scenery grows wilder as they go.

## Route
The first days follow gentle farmland and woodland to Drymen, then climb over Conic Hill for a first view of Loch Lomond. The route traces the long eastern shore of the loch, where the path is rough and rooty for several hours. Beyond Tyndrum it follows an old military road across Rannoch Moor, a vast, empty bog plateau. From the Kingshouse the trail climbs the Devil's Staircase, its highest point at about 550 m, then drops to Kinlochleven. The final day crosses the Lairigmor valley and reaches Fort William at the foot of Ben Nevis, which some walkers climb as a bonus day.

## When to go
April, May and September are the favourite months. Summer is busier and brings midges, tiny biting insects that are worst on still, damp evenings near water; a head net and repellent are essential from June to August. Winter walking is possible but daylight is very short.

## Where to stay
There are hotels, B&Bs, hostels and campsites in every overnight village. Wild camping is generally allowed in Scotland under the outdoor access code, but seasonal camping management zones apply along the east side of Loch Lomond, where a permit is required in summer. Baggage transfer companies will move bags between accommodations each day.

## Difficulty
This is a moderate walk with no technical terrain. Daily distances of 15 to 30 km are the main challenge, together with rain and wet ground. It is a good first multi-day trek.

## Practicalities
Milngavie is a short train ride from Glasgow, and Fort William has rail and bus connections back south. The currency is the pound sterling. Waterproofs are the most important kit item: rain is likely on several days in any season.

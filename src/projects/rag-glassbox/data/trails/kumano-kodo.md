# Kumano Kodo

## Overview
The Kumano Kodo is a network of ancient pilgrimage paths on the Kii Peninsula, south of Osaka in Japan. The routes lead to the three grand shrines of Kumano and have been walked for more than a thousand years, by emperors as well as ordinary pilgrims. They are part of the UNESCO World Heritage site Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range, listed in 2004.

## Route
The most popular route is the Nakahechi, the Imperial route. Walkers usually start at Takijiri-oji, near Tanabe, and reach the grand shrine of Kumano Hongu Taisha after about 38 km, normally in two or three days. The trail climbs through forests of cedar and cypress on stone steps and old cobbled paths, passing small subsidiary shrines called oji. From Hongu, pilgrims can continue to the other grand shrines, Kumano Hayatama Taisha and Kumano Nachi Taisha, next to the tall Nachi waterfall.

## When to go
Spring and autumn are the best seasons, with mild temperatures and cherry blossom or autumn leaves. The rainy season in June and early July brings heavy rain that makes stone steps slippery. Summer is hot and humid, and typhoons can affect the region from August to October.

## Where to stay
The traditional choice is a minshuku, a family-run guesthouse, or a ryokan inn, where dinner and breakfast are included and guests sleep on futons. Accommodation in the small mountain villages is limited, so it must be reserved in advance, often through the local tourism bureau. The hot spring village of Yunomine Onsen, near Hongu, is a popular place to rest.

## Difficulty
The Nakahechi is moderate. The distances are short, but there are steep climbs and descents, and the path can be muddy and slippery after rain. Navigation is easy thanks to numbered markers every 500 m.

## Practicalities
Pilgrims can collect stamps at oji shrines in a stamp book. Walkers who complete both the Kumano Kodo and the Camino de Santiago can register as Dual Pilgrims, a twinning between the two routes. Local buses connect the trailheads with Tanabe and Kii-Tanabe station, and luggage transfer is available between inns.

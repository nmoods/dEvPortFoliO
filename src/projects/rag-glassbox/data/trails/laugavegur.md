# Laugavegur

## Overview
The Laugavegur trail crosses the Icelandic highlands for about 55 km between Landmannalaugar and Þórsmörk. It is usually walked in three or four days. The landscape changes every few hours, from coloured rhyolite mountains and steaming vents to black lava fields, glaciers and a green birch valley.

## Route
Landmannalaugar, the northern start, is known for its natural hot spring and the orange, pink and green rhyolite hills around it. The first day climbs past fumaroles to the hut at Hrafntinnusker, the highest point of the trail at about 1,100 m, where snowfields remain well into summer. The route then descends to Álftavatn lake, crosses a black sand desert to Emstrur and finishes in the sheltered valley of Þórsmörk. Many walkers add the Fimmvörðuháls pass, about 25 km more, climbing between two glaciers and descending past dozens of waterfalls to Skógar on the south coast.

## When to go
The trail is officially open from late June to early September, when the highland roads and huts open. Outside that window there are no buses and conditions can be dangerous. Even in July, storms with strong wind and sleet are possible, and fog can make route-finding difficult on the snowfields.

## Where to stay
Huts at each stage are run by Ferðafélag Íslands, the Touring Association of Iceland, and must be booked well in advance. Campsites are available next to every hut. Camping elsewhere along the trail is not permitted, to protect the fragile vegetation.

## Difficulty
The walking is moderate, but the weather makes it serious. Several glacial rivers must be forded on foot; water is ice cold and fastest in the afternoon, so crossings are easier in the morning. Sandals or water shoes and walking poles help.

## Practicalities
Highland buses connect Reykjavík with Landmannalaugar and Þórsmörk in summer. There are no shops on the trail, so all food must be carried. Registering your trip with the Icelandic safety website before leaving is recommended.
